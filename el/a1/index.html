Was Griechischsprachige schon können

Your head start: ο, η, το = der, die, das

No language in this app is closer to German grammar than Greek: three genders with the article in front (ο / η / το = der / die / das), cases that change the article (ο άντρας → τον άντρα = der Mann → den Mann), verbs that change for each person, a polite εσείς (= Sie) and a past with „have": έχω δουλέψει = ich habe gearbeitet. And German is full of Greek words: Telefon, Musik, Problem, Theater. What is new: the verb in position 2 and at the end. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · προφορά

German sounds, written in Greek letters

Read the right-hand column as Greek. German stresses the first syllable of most words: Ar-beit, Fa-mi-lie is an exception (Fa-mi-lie).

GermanSounds likeExampleRead it as
Achtung!Latin letters that look Greek. German B = μπ (not β), H = a soft breath (not η), P = π (not ρ), X = ξ (not χ), Y = ü (not υ). German Z = τσ, not the Greek ζ! Read every letter as Latin: Pizza = „πίτσα", Haus = „(χ)άους".
Your bonusGreek χ gives you both German ch sounds for free: Buch = „μπουχ" like χαρά, and ich = „ιχ" like χέρι. Only sch, ü and ö are new: practise „sch" like English „sh", and round your lips for ü and ö.
2 · Satzbau · σύνταξη

Verb 2nd, and the second verb at the end

Greek word order is free; German is strict. In a German main clause the conjugated verb must stay in position 2, and every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Δουλεύω σήμερα.
Heute arbeite ich.Σήμερα δουλεύω. If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Πρέπει να δουλέψω σήμερα. The second verb goes to the end, and there is no „να".
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Σηκώνομαι στις 7. „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Griechenland?Είστε από την Ελλάδα; German marks the question with the verb first (and writes ?, not ;).
Say „ich"Greek drops the subject (Δουλεύω σήμερα), because the verb ending shows the person. German almost never does: Ich arbeite heute. Every German sentence needs a subject, even „es": Es regnet = Βρέχει.
Sie, du, ihrGerman du = εσύ, ihr = εσείς (several friends), Sie = εσείς (polite, always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say εσείς out of respect.
3 · Artikel & Genus · γένος

der, die, das = ο, η, το

Greek and German work the same way: three genders, article in front, and you learn the article with every noun. Often the gender even matches: der Mann / ο άντρας, die Tür / η πόρτα, das Kind / το παιδί. When it doesn't match, take extra care. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

PluralGreek has οι / τα in the plural; German has only one plural article: die for all genders: die Männer, die Frauen, die Kinder. The noun changes too: das Kind → die Kinder, der Tisch → die Tische, das Auto → die Autos.
4 · Fälle · πτώσεις

Cases: the article changes, just like in Greek

You already know this: ο άντρας, τον άντρα, του άντρα. German does the same with der, den, des, and adds one more case, the Dativ (dem). Even the -ν matches: τον / den!

FallGermanΕλληνικάClue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.ο άντρας είναι εδώ.ονομαστική
AkkusativIch sehe den Mann.Βλέπω τον άντρα.αιτιατική → den
DativIch gebe dem Mann das Buch.Δίνω το βιβλίο στον άντρα.„σε" (to whom) → dem
Genitivdas Auto des Mannesτο αυτοκίνητο του άντραγενική → des
Your clueWhere Greek says σε + someone (στον άντρα) or uses a genitive pronoun (μου δίνει), German uses the Dativ: Er gibt mir das Buch = Μου δίνει το βιβλίο. Everyday German often replaces the Genitiv with „von": das Auto von Nikos.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Nikos Georgiou ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Thessaloniki in Griechenland. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Nikos wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Ο φίλος σου ο Νίκος θέλει να γραφτεί σε μαθήματα γερμανικών. Γράψε τα 5 στοιχεία που λείπουν.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (όνομα) and Familienname / Nachname (επώνυμο). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Griechenland", „aus Zypern", with no article. Write names in Latin letters, as in your passport. Dates are written day.month.year, as in Greece.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Greek speakers, because some Latin letters look Greek: B is „μπε" (not β), H is „χα" (not η), P is „πε" (not ρ), X is „ιξ" (not χ), Y is „ύψιλον". Also: C is „τσε", D is „ντε", G is „γκε", J is „γιοτ", R is „ερ" from the throat, V is „φάου", W is „βε" and Z is „τσετ". Tap a letter to hear it.

Greek names · ελληνικά ονόματα

Latin spellingYour passport shows your name in Latin letters: use exactly that spelling. Greek names can be written several ways (Giorgos, Yiorgos, Georgios): always use the passport version.
