Warum Ukrainischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

Ukrainian already has three genders, cases (German has four, you have seven) and many German loanwords: дах (Dach), фарба (Farbe), рахунок (Rechnung), шухляда (Schublade), майстер, бухгалтер, штраф. And your г is almost exactly the German h! Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · вимова

German sounds, written in Ukrainian letters

Many German spellings are Ukrainian sounds in disguise: German sch = ш, tsch = ч, z = ц, w = в, ch = х, h = г and g = ґ. Read the right-hand column as Ukrainian and you will be very close.

Achtung!German ei = ай, eu = ой, ie = довге і: mein = „майн", heute = „гойте", Liebe = „ліібе". And German g is always ґ, never г: gut = „ґут", Tag = „так".
Long vowelsUkrainian has no long vowels; German does, and they change the meaning: Staat (state) vs. Stadt (city), Miete (rent) vs. Mitte (middle). A double vowel, ie or h means long. And two new vowels: ü (say і with rounded lips, not „ю") and ö (say е with rounded lips).
Hard endingsUkrainian keeps final consonants voiced (хліб, дуб). German makes them voiceless: Hund = „хунт", gelb = „ґельп", Tag = „так". And German usually stresses the first syllable of the stem: AR-bei-ten, FAH-ren.
2 · Satzbau · порядок слів

Ukrainian: free order. German: the verb is always 2nd

Thanks to cases, Ukrainian word order is free. German is strict: in a main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2, whatever comes first. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Я працюю сьогодні.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2 and the subject moves behind it. Never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Я мушу сьогодні працювати. (the infinitive goes to the end)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Я встаю о 7. The prefix auf- jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus der Ukraine?Ви з України? Ukrainian uses чи or intonation; German puts the verb first. And note: „aus der Ukraine", with an article!
Always a verb and a subjectUkrainian says „я вдома" with no verb and „холодно" with no subject. German needs both: Ich bin zu Hause, es ist kalt, es regnet (іде дощ).
Sie = ВиGerman Sie works like your polite Ви, with a capital S and a plural verb: Sie sind. ihr = ви (plural, informal): ihr seid.
3 · Artikel & Genus · рід

Three genders, like Ukrainian, but German shows them with an article

Ukrainian has no articles: the ending shows the gender (дім, жінка, вікно). German puts der, die or das in front. Often the gender matches, but not always.

Two articlesGerman also has ein / eine (a, one) and der / die / das (the). Ukrainian has neither, so you will forget them at first. Rule of thumb: first mention = ein, already known = der. -ung, -heit, -keit = always die, -chen = always das.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Andrij Kowalenko ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Charkiw in der Ukraine. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Andrij wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Твій друг Андрій хоче записатися на курс німецької. Впиши 5 відсутніх даних.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (ім'я) and Familienname / Nachname (прізвище). There is no field for the patronymic (по батькові).. The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Careful: Ukraine takes an article in German: „aus der Ukraine", „in der Ukraine". So „Herkunftsland: Ukraine", but „Ich komme aus der Ukraine".
