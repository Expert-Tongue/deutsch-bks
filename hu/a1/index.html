Was Ungarischsprachige schon können

Your head start: ö, ü, fél három and separable verbs

Hungarian and German are not related, but they have lived side by side for centuries, and it shows: you already say ö and ü, you already say fél három (= halb drei), you already split verbs like German (felkel → Hatkor kelek fel = Ich stehe um sechs auf), you build long compound words in the same order (munka + szerződés = Arbeits + vertrag), and you use many German words: vicc, pech, cetli, sógor. What is new: der, die, das and the verb in position 2. Tap any to hear the German.

Achtung!The s trap. German s is never Hungarian s (= sh)! At the start before a vowel it is Hungarian z (Sonne = „zone"), elsewhere it is sz (Haus = „hausz"). German sch = Hungarian s, German z = Hungarian c, German w = v, German v = f.
Your bonusö and ü, the hardest sounds for most learners, are native to you. German long vowels work like Hungarian accents: Tag = „ták", vier = „fír", Schule = „súle". Only German a needs care: always open like á, never the round Hungarian a.
2 · Satzbau · szórend

Verb 2nd, and the second verb at the end

Hungarian word order follows the focus and is very free. German is strict: the conjugated verb stays in position 2, and every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Ma dolgozom.
Heute arbeite ich.Ma dolgozom. If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Ma dolgoznom kell. The second verb goes to the end.
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Hétkor kelek fel. Just like Hungarian: „auf" separates, like „fel".
Kommen Sie aus Ungarn?Magyarországról jön? German marks the question with the verb first.
Say „ich"Hungarian drops the subject (Ma dolgozom), because the verb ending shows the person. German almost never does: Ich arbeite heute. Every German sentence needs a subject, even „es": Es regnet = Esik.
Sie, du, ihrGerman du = te, ihr = ti, Sie = Ön / Maga (always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say Ön.
3 · Artikel & Genus · nemek

der, die, das: the one thing Hungarian doesn't have

Hungarian has no grammatical gender at all: ő = he and she, and the article a / az is the same for every noun. German has three genders and three articles: der Tisch, die Katze, das Buch. Learn every noun with its article, as one word. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

PluralHungarian always adds -k; German has several plural endings: das Kind → die Kinder, der Tisch → die Tische, die Frau → die Frauen, das Auto → die Autos. And after numbers German uses the plural: zwei Kinder (két gyerek, without -k!).
4 · Fälle · esetek

Cases: Hungarian has about 18, German only four

Hungarian adds endings to the noun: a férfi, a férfit, a férfinak. German changes the article in front: der Mann, den Mann, dem Mann. Same idea, other side of the noun, and far fewer forms. At A1, focus on Nominativ and Akkusativ: only der changes, der → den.

Reading practice

Scan for key words before you read the statement. Negations (nicht, kein, nur, geschlossen, verboten) are the most common traps.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Gábor Kovács ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Debrecen in Ungarn. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Gábor wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. A barátod, Gábor be akar iratkozni egy németkurzusra. Írd be az 5 hiányzó adatot.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (keresztnév) and Familienname / Nachname (vezetéknév). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Ungarn", with no article. Careful with name order: Vorname = keresztnév (Gábor), Familienname = vezetéknév (Kovács). Dates are written day.month.year: 23.12.2026, the reverse of Hungarian 2026.12.23.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Hungarian speakers: J is „jot", R is „er" from the throat, S is „esz" (never Hungarian s!), V is „fau", W is „vé", Y is „üpszilon" and Z is „cet". Good news: C is „cé" and H is „há", just like Hungarian! Tap a letter to hear it.
