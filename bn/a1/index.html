Was Bengalischsprachige schon können

Your head start: an Indo-European cousin with the verb at the end

Bengali and German are distant cousins in the Indo-European family: নাম = Name, নব = neu, দাঁত = Zahn, তিন = drei. Your verbs already change for each person (আমি করি, তুমি করো, সে করে), you already have a polite „you" (আপনি = Sie), you already mark objects (মাকে, বাবার) and you already put the verb at the end (আমি আজ কাজ করি). What is new: der, die, das and the verb in position 2. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · উচ্চারণ

German sounds, written in Bengali letters

Read the right-hand column as Bengali. German stresses the first syllable of most words: Ar-beit, Fa-mi-lie is an exception (Fa-mi-lie).

Achtung!z, f, v, w do not exist in Bengali. Bengali often says জ for z, ফ (ph) for f, ভ (bh) for v and w. In German they are four separate sounds: Zug = „ৎসুক", Fisch with the upper teeth on the lip, Wasser = „ভ়াসার" with the teeth on the lip too, but voiced. And German s at the start sounds like English z.
The vowel at the endBengali অ is often said like a short o (কলম = kolom). German a is always open, like আ: Mann, not „মন". And a final -e is always pronounced, short and soft: bitte, Schule.
2 · Satzbau · বাক্যগঠন

Bengali: verb at the end. German: verb 2nd, the rest at the end

Bengali puts the verb last: আমি আজ কাজ করি. In a German main clause the conjugated verb stands in position 2. But every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end, exactly where Bengali puts it.

Ich arbeite heute.আমি আজ কাজ করি।
Heute arbeite ich.আজ আমি কাজ করি। If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.আজ আমাকে কাজ করতে হবে। The second verb goes to the end.
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.আমি সাতটায় ঘুম থেকে উঠি। „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Bangladesch?আপনি কি বাংলাদেশ থেকে এসেছেন? No কি: the verb goes first.
Verb endingsYou know this already: আমি করি, তুমি করো, সে করে, আপনি করেন. German: ich komme, du kommst, er kommt, wir kommen. Even the polite form ends in -n in both: আপনি করেন, Sie kommen!
Sie, du, ihrGerman has du (তুমি), ihr (তোমরা) and Sie (আপনি, always with a capital S). There is no তুই: use du with friends and Sie with everyone you would call আপনি.
3 · Artikel & Genus · লিঙ্গ

der, die, das: the one thing Bengali doesn't have

Bengali nouns have no gender (সে = he and she!), and the classifier -টা / -টি is the same for every noun. German has three genders and shows them with an article in front: der Tisch, die Katze, das Buch. Learn every noun with its article, as one word. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

GermanGenderবাংলাMemory trick

PluralBengali adds -রা, -গুলো or -দের; German has several plural endings: das Kind → die Kinder, der Tisch → die Tische, die Frau → die Frauen, das Auto → die Autos. Learn the plural with the noun: der Tisch, die Tische.
4 · Fälle · কারক

You already know cases: -কে and -র

Bengali adds an ending after the noun: লোকটাকে দেখি, লোকটার গাড়ি. German changes the article before the noun: den Mann, des Mannes. Same idea, other side. At A1, focus on Nominativ and Akkusativ: only der changes, der → den.

FallGermanবাংলাClue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.লোকটা এখানে আছে।no ending
AkkusativIch sehe den Mann.আমি লোকটাকে দেখি।-কে (whom) → den
DativIch gebe dem Mann das Buch.আমি লোকটাকে বইটা দিই।-কে (to whom) → dem
Genitivdas Auto des Mannesলোকটার গাড়ি-র → des
Your clueBengali uses -কে for both „whom" and „to whom". German splits it: the thing you give is Akkusativ, the person who receives it is Dativ: Ich gebe dem Mann (Dativ) das Buch (Akkusativ). For -র, everyday German simply says „von": das Auto von Arif = আরিফের গাড়ি.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Arif Hossain ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Dhaka in Bangladesch. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Arif wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. আপনার বন্ধু আরিফ জার্মান কোর্সে ভর্তি হতে চায়। বাদ পড়া ৫টি তথ্য লিখুন।

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (নাম) and Familienname / Nachname (পদবি). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Bangladesch", „aus Indien", with no article. Write your name exactly as in your passport, even if it has only one name or „Md.". Dates are written day.month.year, as at home.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Bengali speakers, who know the English letter names: A is „আ" (not „এ"), E is „এ" (not „ই"), I is „ঈ" (not „আই"), C is „ৎসে", G is „গে" (not „জি"), H is „হা" (not „এইচ"), J is „ইয়ট" (not „জে"), R is „এর" from the throat, U is „উ" (not „ইউ"), V is „ফাউ", W is „ভে", Y is „ইউপসিলন" and Z is „ৎসেট". Tap a letter to hear it.

Bengali names · বাংলা নাম

Md. and one-word namesIf your passport says „Md." or „Mohammad", write it exactly so. If you have only one name, German forms may show it as the Familienname: ask the office how they want it.
Spelling lettersSpell your name as in your passport: Hossain, Hussain and Hosen are three different names for a German office. Say „Doppel-" for double letters: Hossain = H – O – Doppel-S – A – I – N.
Mein Name ist Rahman.R – A – H – M – A – N: „এর – আ – হা – এম – আ – এন" (careful: A = „আ", H = „হা")
