Warum Türkischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

Turkish uses the Latin alphabet, already has the German sounds ö and ü, puts the verb at the end (German does that too, half of the time), and marks cases with endings, so the idea of cases is not new to you. This page turns those into shortcuts. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · telaffuz

German sounds, written the Turkish way

Turkish is written as it is spoken; German often uses several letters for one sound. Read the right-hand column as Turkish and you will be close.

Achtung!Letters that look the same but sound different: German z = ts (not Turkish z), German v = f, German w = Turkish v, German j = Turkish y, German c appears mostly in ch/ck. And Turkish c (= dsch) does not exist in German spelling: Dschungel, Job.
Achtung!No extra vowel before st-, sp-, sch-. Turkish adds one (İstanbul, istasyon, ispanak). German doesn't: Straße, Stadt, Sprache, Schule. Start directly with the consonant. And German devoices final consonants: Tag sounds like „tak", und like „unt".
2 · Satzbau · kelime sırası

Turkish: verb at the end. German: verb 2nd, the rest at the end

In Turkish the verb always comes last. In a German main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2. But a second verb, a participle, or the verb after weil / dass goes to the end, exactly where Turkish puts it.

Ich arbeite heute.Ben bugün çalışıyorum.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2, the subject moves behind it.
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Bugün çalışmam lazım. (infinitive at the end, close to Turkish)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.The prefix auf- jumps to the end. Saat 7'de kalkıyorum.
Kommen Sie aus der Türkei?Yes/no question: verb first. Turkish uses mi: Türkiye'den mi geliyorsunuz?
Sie = sizFormal „Sie" works like your siz, but the verb is in the 3rd person plural: Sie sind (siz …sınız), Sie haben. Always with a capital S.
3 · Artikel & Genus · dilbilgisel cinsiyet

Turkish has no gender. German has three.

In Turkish o means he, she and it, and nouns have no gender. German has der, die, das and er / sie / es. This is the biggest new habit: learn every noun with its article, and practise er (he) and sie (she) until they are automatic.

GermanTurkishTip
der Mann · eradam · omen, days, months, seasons are mostly der
die Frau · siekadın · owords ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft are always die

die Kinderçocuklarall plurals use die
TrickGerman ein / eine is your bir. But German also has der / die / das for "the", which Turkish doesn't need. Colour your notes: blue der, red die, green das.
4 · Fälle · hâller

You already use cases: -(y)i, -(y)e, -in

Turkish marks cases with endings on the noun. German marks them mostly on the article. The ideas are the same.

FallGermanTurkishYour clue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.Adam burada.yalın hâl
AkkusativIch sehe den Mann.Adamı görüyorum.-(y)i → den
DativIch gebe dem Mann das Buch.Adama kitabı veriyorum.-(y)e → dem

Achtung!German needs extra small words where Turkish uses endings: Turkish ev-de (in the house) = German im Haus, ev-den = aus dem Haus. At A2 you'll see how -e and -de match German Akkusativ and Dativ after in, an, auf.

Lesen · okuma

Reading practice

Scan for key words before you read the statement. Negations (nicht, kein, nur, geschlossen, verboten) are the most common traps.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Mehmet Demir ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Izmir in der Türkei. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Mehmet wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Arkadaşınız Mehmet Almanca kursuna kaydolmak istiyor. Eksik 5 bilgiyi yazın.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (ad) and Familienname / Nachname (soyadı). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: die Türkei, so „Herkunftsland: Türkei".
