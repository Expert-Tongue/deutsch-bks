Warum Arabischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

Arabic already has the German sounds that English speakers find hardest: ch (خ) and the throaty r (غ). Standard Arabic has grammatical gender, cases (مرفوع، منصوب، مجرور), "the ones come first" numbers (واحد وعشرون = einundzwanzig) and the same "my head hurts" structure. German also took many words from Arabic: Zucker, Kaffee, Sofa, Matratze. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · النطق

German sounds, written in Arabic letters

German writes from left to right and writes every vowel. Read the right-hand column as Arabic and you will be close.

GermanSounds likeExampleRead it as
p ≠ bArabic has no p, so Paar (pair) and Bar (bar), packen and backen sound the same to many Arabic speakers. For p, blow a puff of air: hold a paper in front of your mouth and make it move. The same for v/f and w: German w = ڤ (Wasser), not و.
e, o, ü, öArabic has three vowels (a, i, u); German has many more. e as in Tee and o as in rot are long and clear. ü = say ي with rounded lips, ö = say e with rounded lips. Bett (bed) and Bitte (please) must sound different.
No extra vowelGerman words can start with two or three consonants: Straße, Sprache, Stuhl. Don't add a vowel before or between them: it is „Schtraße", not „Isschtraße" or „Sitraße".
2 · Satzbau · ترتيب الجملة

Arabic often starts with the verb. German puts it 2nd

Arabic can start with the verb (ذهب الولد) or the noun (الولد ذهب). In a German main clause the conjugated verb is always in position 2. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.أعمل اليوم.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2 and the subject moves behind it.
Ich muss heute arbeiten.يجب أن أعمل اليوم. (the infinitive goes to the end)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.أستيقظ الساعة السابعة. The prefix auf- jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Syrien?هل حضرتك من سوريا؟ German has no هل: the verb comes first.
The verb "sein"Arabic needs no verb in أنا متعب. German always does: Ich bin müde. Das ist gut. And it always needs a subject: Es regnet (تمطر).
Sie and duSie = حضرتك, always with a capital S and the "they" verb (Sie sind). du = أنتَ and أنتِ: German doesn't change the word or the verb for a man or a woman.
3 · Artikel & Genus · المذكر والمؤنث

Arabic has two genders. German has three

You already know that nouns have gender. German adds a third, neuter (das), and shows the gender with an article: der, die, das. Arabic ـة often means feminine; German endings like -ung or -heit do the same.

der = الGerman der / die / das works like ال ("the"), and ein / eine like tanwīn ("a"): كتابٌ = ein Buch, الكتاب = das Buch. -ung, -heit, -keit = always die, -chen = always das.
4 · Fälle · الإعراب

Your إعراب helps: German has cases too

Standard Arabic marks case on the noun ending (ـُ ـَ ـِ). German marks it mostly on the article. Three German cases match your three; the Dativ is new and often matches لِـ.

Same as ArabicGerman has "to me" sentences: Mir ist kalt = أشعر بالبرد, Das gefällt mir = هذا يعجبني, Mir geht es gut = أنا بخير. mir = لي، ـني.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Omar Haddad ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Aleppo in Syrien. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Omar wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. صديقك عمر يريد التسجيل في دورة ألمانية. اكتب المعلومات الخمس الناقصة.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (الاسم الأول) and Familienname / Nachname (اسم العائلة). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country names usually have no article: „aus Syrien", „aus Ägypten". Exceptions: „aus dem Irak", „aus dem Libanon", „aus dem Jemen".

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps if you learned the English alphabet: A is „a", E is „e", I is „i", H is „ha", J is „jot", R is „er", V is „fau", W is „ve", Y is „üpsilon", Z is „tset". And keep P and B apart! Tap a letter to hear it.
