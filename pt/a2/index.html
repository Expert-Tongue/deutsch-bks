Warum A2 für Portugiesischsprachige leichter ist

A lot of A2 grammar already exists in Portuguese

A2 is the level where German grammar opens up: the past tense, two-way prepositions, subordinate clauses, reflexive verbs, comparisons and polite forms. Portuguese already has reflexive verbs (lavo-me), a conditional for politeness (poderia, gostaria) and the same "where to / where" logic as German. The new part is word order, and one big trap in the past tense.

1 · Perfekt · pretérito perfeito

Ich habe gearbeitet = trabalhei (not „tenho trabalhado"!)

German builds its everyday past with a helper + participle. It looks like Portuguese "tenho trabalhado", but it means a finished action: trabalhei. Most verbs use haben, movement or change use sein. The helper stands in position 2, the participle at the end.

Sie ist zu Hause geblieben.Ela ficou em casa.seinexception: bleiben, sein
Perfekt or Präteritum?Spoken German uses the Perfekt for almost every past event: „Gestern habe ich gearbeitet" = ontem trabalhei. For "tenho trabalhado muito" (ongoing) German uses the present: „Ich arbeite in letzter Zeit viel."

2 · Wechselpräpositionen · aonde ou onde?

Akkusativ = para onde, Dativ = onde

Portuguese already separates the questions: para onde vais / vai? (movement) and onde estás / está? (place). German uses the same idea with nine prepositions: Wohin? → Akkusativ, Wo? → Dativ. Portuguese often uses "em" for both; German changes the article.

Wohin? → Akkusativpara onde?Wo? → Dativonde?
Ich gehe in die Stadt.Vou à cidade.Ich bin in der Stadt.Estou na cidade.
Ich lege das Buch auf den Tisch.Ponho o livro na mesa.Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.O livro está na mesa.
Wir fahren an den See.Vamos ao lago.Wir sind am See.Estamos no lago.
Die Katze läuft unter das Bett.O gato corre para debaixo da cama.Die Katze schläft unter dem Bett.O gato dorme debaixo da cama.
Nine wordsin, an, auf, unter, über, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen. „Ponho o livro na mesa" and „o livro está na mesa" use the same word in Portuguese. In German, ask: is there movement (wohin?) or not (wo?)?
3 · Nebensätze · orações subordinadas

After weil, dass, wenn: the verb goes to the end

This is the biggest change for Portuguese speakers. After porque or que, Portuguese keeps the normal order. In a German subordinate clause the conjugated verb goes to the last position.

Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin.Não venho porque estou doente.
Ich glaube, dass er heute kommt.Acho que ele vem hoje.
Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an.Se eu tiver tempo, ligo-te / te ligo.
Ich weiß nicht, ob sie zu Hause ist.Não sei se ela está em casa.
Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren.Embora esteja a chover / chovendo, vamos passear.
Verb, comma, verbIf the sentence starts with the clause (Wenn ich Zeit habe, …), the main clause starts with the verb: „rufe ich dich an". Portuguese says "se tiver tempo, ligo-te"; German says „wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an".
4 · Reflexive Verben · verbos reflexivos

mich, dich, sich = me, te, se

Good news: this works almost exactly like Portuguese. German puts mich / dich / sich after the verb, like Portugal (lavo-me); Brazil puts it before (eu me lavo).

Achtung!Not every verb matches: sich beeilen = apressar-se ✓, sich freuen = alegrar-se ✓, but aufstehen = levantar-se has no sich in German, and sich duschen = tomar banho has one. Learn each verb with or without sich.

Like melhorPortuguese has a few irregular ones (melhor, pior, maior); German has a few too: gut → besser, viel → mehr, gern → lieber. Short words often add an umlaut: alt → älter, groß → größer, jung → jünger.
6 · Höflich sein · cortesia

würde, hätte, könnte = o condicional

Portuguese is polite with the conditional and imperfect: poderia…?, queria…, gostaria…. German does exactly the same with three short verbs. They sound much friendlier than the present tense, so use them at offices, at the doctor's and in the exam.

Ich hätte gern einen Termin.Queria marcar uma consulta.
Könnten Sie mir helfen?Poderia ajudar-me / me ajudar?
Ich würde gern am Montag kommen.Gostaria de vir na segunda-feira.
An deiner Stelle würde ich zum Arzt gehen.No teu / seu lugar, eu ia ao médico.
Cultural tipGerman communication is direct: „Nein" is not rude, and Germans say clearly what they think. Small talk is shorter, people keep more physical distance, and greetings are a handshake rather than beijinhos. Be on time: 5 minutes late is already late.
