Warum A2 für Englischsprachige leichter ist

Much of A2 grammar has an English twin

A2 is where German grammar opens up: the past tense, two-way prepositions, subordinate clauses, reflexive verbs, comparisons and polite forms. English has a past with "have" (I have worked), comparatives with -er (faster) and polite "would" and "could". The two new parts are the verb at the end and the article changing after prepositions.

1 · Perfekt · the spoken past

Ich habe gearbeitet = I worked

It looks like the English present perfect ("I have worked"), but German uses it for finished past events, even with "yesterday": Ich habe gestern gearbeitet = I worked yesterday. Most verbs use haben; verbs of movement or change use sein (to be). The helper stands in position 2, the participle at the end.

"Is gone"Old English used "be" for movement too, and you can still hear it: "he is gone", "Christ is risen". German kept it: er ist gegangen. Many participles look like English ones with ge-: gekommen (come), gefunden (found), gesungen (sung), getrunken (drunk).

2 · Wechselpräpositionen · into or in?

Akkusativ = movement, Dativ = place

English often shows movement with a different preposition: into the city vs. in the city, onto the table vs. on the table. German uses the same preposition but changes the article: Wohin? (where to?) → Akkusativ, Wo? (where?) → Dativ.

Wohin? → AkkusativEnglishWo? → DativEnglish
Ich gehe in die Stadt.I'm going into town.Ich bin in der Stadt.I'm in town.
Ich lege das Buch auf den Tisch.I put the book onto the table.Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.The book is on the table.
Wir fahren an den See.We're going to the lake.Wir sind am See.We're at the lake.
Die Katze läuft unter das Bett.The cat runs under the bed.Die Katze schläft unter dem Bett.The cat sleeps under the bed.
Nine wordsin, an, auf, unter, über, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen. Ask yourself: could I say "into / onto" in English? Then it's Akkusativ. „Ich gehe ins Kino" (into the cinema) vs. „Ich bin im Kino" (in the cinema).
3 · Nebensätze · subordinate clauses

After weil, dass, wenn: the verb goes to the end

This is the biggest change for English speakers. After "because" or "that", English keeps the normal order. In a German subordinate clause the conjugated verb goes to the last position.

Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin.I'm not coming because I am ill.
Ich glaube, dass er heute kommt.I think (that) he is coming today. German never drops „dass".
Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an.If I have time, I'll call you.
Ich weiß nicht, ob sie zu Hause ist.I don't know whether she is at home.
Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren.Although it's raining, we're going for a walk.
Verb, comma, verbIf the sentence starts with the clause, the main clause starts with the verb: „Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an." English says "If I have time, I'll call you"; German puts the two verbs side by side around the comma.
4 · Reflexive Verben · reflexive verbs

mich, dich, sich = myself, yourself, himself

English uses "myself" only for emphasis (I cut myself). German uses reflexive pronouns with many everyday verbs, where English has none: Ich ziehe mich an = I get dressed.

Achtung!Learn these with sich: sich setzen (sit down), sich beeilen (hurry), sich freuen (be pleased), sich erinnern (remember), sich fühlen (feel). „Ich fühle mich gut" = I feel good.
5 · Komparation · comparison

-er and am -sten, just like -er and -est

PositivKomparativSuperlativEnglish
schnellschnelleram schnellstenfast – faster – fastest

gutbesseram bestengood – better – best

London ist größer als Berlin.London is bigger than Berlin. als = than (not "as")
Mein Bruder ist so groß wie ich.My brother is as tall as me. so … wie = as … as
Unlike EnglishGerman never says "more expensive": every adjective takes -er, even long ones: teurer, interessanter, komplizierter. Short words often add an umlaut: alt → älter, groß → größer, jung → jünger.
6 · Höflich sein · politeness

würde, hätte, könnte = would, would have, could

English softens requests with could you…?, I'd like… and I would…. German does exactly the same with three short verbs. They sound much friendlier than the present tense, so use them at offices, at the doctor's and in the exam.

Ich hätte gern einen Termin.I'd like an appointment.
Könnten Sie mir helfen?Could you help me?
Ich würde gern am Montag kommen.I'd like to come on Monday.
An deiner Stelle würde ich zum Arzt gehen.If I were you, I'd go to the doctor.
Cultural tipGerman communication is direct. „Nein" is not rude, and Germans rarely soften criticism the British way ("that's quite interesting…"). „Das ist nicht gut" means exactly that. Small talk is shorter, and „Wie geht's?" is a real question, so expect a real answer. Be on time: 5 minutes late is already late.

Hören · listening

Listening practice

Read the question first, then press „Anhören". Your browser reads the text in German. Open „Text" only after you have answered. At A2 the speakers use the Perfekt a lot: listen for habe / bin + participle.

Lesen · reading

Reading practice

Read the question first, then look for the key words in the text. Watch for nicht, kein, nur, schon, noch and for comparatives (größer, teurer).
