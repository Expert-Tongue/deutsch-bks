Warum Englischsprachige Vorteile haben

English and German are sisters

English and German both come from the same West Germanic family, so thousands of everyday words are cousins: Haus (house), Wasser (water), Buch (book), trinken (drink), gut (good). Once you know the sound shifts below, you can guess many new words. What English lost over the centuries, German kept: genders, cases and a strict word order. That is where your work lies. 1 · Aussprache · pronunciation

German is spelled the way it sounds

Good news: unlike English, German spelling is regular. Learn these rules once and you can read any word aloud. Three new vowels: ä (like e in "bed"), ö (say "ay" with rounded lips) and ü (say "ee" with rounded lips).

Achtung!w is "v", v is "f", z is "ts". These three cause most English-speaker accents: Wasser = „vasser", Vater = „fahter", Zeit = „tsite". Get them right and you instantly sound more German.

Say every letterGerman has almost no silent letters: the k in Knie, the p in Psychologie and the final e in bitte („bittuh") are all spoken.
2 · Satzbau · word order

English: subject, then verb. German: the verb is always 2nd

In a German main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2, whatever comes first. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.I work today.
Heute arbeite ich."Today I work" is wrong in German: the verb stays in position 2, so the subject moves behind it. Never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.I must work today. The infinitive goes to the very end.
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.I get up at 7. Like a phrasal verb, but „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus England?Do you come from England? No "do" in German: the verb simply goes first.
No "do", no "-ing"German never uses "do" for questions or negatives: Sprechen Sie Deutsch? (Do you speak German?), Ich spreche nicht Deutsch (I don't speak German). And there is no continuous form: ich lerne = I learn and I'm learning.
Sie, du, ihrEnglish "you" is three words: du (one friend), ihr (several friends) and Sie (polite, one or many, always with a capital S). Sie takes the "they" verb: Sie sind.
3 · Artikel & Genus · gender

English has one "the". German has three

Every German noun is masculine, feminine or neuter: der Tisch, die Lampe, das Buch. The gender rarely follows logic (das Mädchen, the girl, is neuter), so learn every noun with its article, like a surname. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

ShortcutsWords ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -tät, -tion are always die: die Zeitung, die Universität, die Information. -chen and -lein are always das. And every plural takes die.
4 · Fälle · cases

You already use cases: he, him, his

English kept cases only in pronouns: he sees, I see him, I give it to him, his car. German does the same with the article in front of every noun.

FallGermanEnglishYour clue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.The man is here.he · who does it
AkkusativIch sehe den Mann.I see the man.him → den
DativIch gebe dem Mann das Buch.I give the man the book.to him → dem

Look: -m and -nCompare whom (Old English "hwām") with German wem, and him with German ihm. The -m ending you still hear in "whom" and "him" is the German Dativ: dem, einem, ihm. At A1, focus on Nominativ and Akkusativ: only der changes, to den.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund James Walker ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Manchester in England. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

James wants to register at the Volkshochschule (the local adult education centre). Write the 5 missing details.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (first name) and Familienname / Nachname (surname / last name). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus England" or „aus Großbritannien", with no article, but „aus den USA" and „aus der Schweiz" take one. Dates are written day.month.year: 03.04.1990 is 3 April, not March 4.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for English speakers: A is „ah" (not „ay"), E is „ay" (not „ee"), I is „ee" (not „eye"), G is a hard „gay" (not „jee"), H is „hah" (not „aitch"), J is „yot" (not „jay"), R is „air", U is „oo" (not „you"), V is „fow" (not „vee"), W is „vay" (not „double-u"), Y is „üpsilon" (not „why") and Z is „tset" (not „zed" or „zee").
