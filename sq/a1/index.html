Was Albanischsprachige schon können

Your head start: cases, genders and „kam punuar"

Albanian already gives you a lot: genders (djali / vajza), cases (nëna → nënës), verbs that change for each person (punoj, punon, punojmë), a polite Ju (= Sie) and a past built with „to have": kam punuar = ich habe gearbeitet. Many letters even sound the same: Albanian j, y, sh, ç and ë help you read German. What is new: the article goes in front (das Haus, not shtëpia), a third gender, and the verb in position 2. Tap any to hear the German.

2 · Satzbau · rendi i fjalëve

Verb 2nd, and the second verb at the end

Albanian and German both often say Ich arbeite heute = Unë punoj sot. But in German the conjugated verb must stay in position 2, and every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Unë punoj sot.
Heute arbeite ich.Sot punoj. If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Duhet të punoj sot. The second verb goes to the end, not right after „muss".
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Ngrihem në orën 7. „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus dem Kosovo?A jeni nga Kosova? No „a": the verb goes first.
Say „ich"Albanian often drops the subject (Punoj sot), because the verb ending shows the person. German almost never does: Ich arbeite heute. Every German sentence needs a subject, even „es": Es regnet = Bie shi.
Sie, du, ihrGerman du = ti, ihr = ju (several friends), Sie = Ju (polite, always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say Ju out of respect.
3 · Artikel & Genus · gjinia

der, die, das: the article moves to the front

Albanian glues the article to the end of the noun: djali, vajza, libri. German puts it in front: der Junge, die Frau, das Buch. Albanian has two genders, German has three, and they often differ: libri is masculine, but das Buch is neuter. Learn every noun with its article. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

4 · Fälle · rasat

You already know cases: Albanian has five

Albanian changes the ending: burri, burrin, burrit. German changes the article: der Mann, den Mann, dem Mann. Same idea, other side of the noun. At A1, focus on Nominativ and Akkusativ: only der changes, der → den.

FallGermanShqipClue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.Burri është këtu.emërore

Your clueWhere Albanian uses the kallëzore (burrin), German uses the Akkusativ; where Albanian uses the dhanore (burrit, nënës), German uses the Dativ. Everyday German often replaces the Genitiv with „von": das Auto von Arben = makina e Arbenit.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Arben Gashi ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Prishtina im Kosovo. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Arben wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Shoku yt Arben dëshiron të regjistrohet në kursin e gjermanishtes. Shkruaj 5 të dhënat që mungojnë.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (emri) and Familienname / Nachname (mbiemri). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus dem Kosovo" (with article!), but „aus Albanien" and „aus Nordmazedonien" with no article. Write names as in your passport. Dates are written day.month.year, as at home.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Albanian speakers, because the German letter name sounds different: H is „ha", J is „jot", Q is „ku" (not Albanian q), R is „er" from the throat, V is „fau", W is „ve", X is „iks" (not Albanian x) and Z is „cet" (not Albanian z). Good news: C is „ce", just like Albanian c! Tap a letter to hear it.

Albanian names · emrat shqip

ë and çGerman keyboards often have no ë or ç. Offices may write Hoxhë as „Hoxhe" or Çeku as „Ceku". Use the spelling in your passport.
