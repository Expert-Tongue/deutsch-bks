Warum Italienischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

German took many words from Italian, especially in banking and music: das Konto, das Risiko, die Kasse, netto, die Oper, das Solo. Italian also gives you German sounds: z sounds like the zz in pizza, sch like the sc in scena, tsch like the ci in ciao. You already know Lei/tu (Sie/du), ho fame (ich habe Hunger) and the passato prossimo with avere and essere. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · pronuncia

German sounds, written the Italian way

Read the right-hand column as Italian and you will be close. Two new vowels: ü (say i with rounded lips) and ö (say e with rounded lips).

Achtung!German words can end in a consonant. Don't add a vowel: Hund = „hunt", not „hunde"; Tag = „tak", not „taga". And the h at the start is breathed: Haus, Hallo, heute. Never silent!
Double lettersIn Italian, a double consonant is held longer (pala / palla). In German it isn't: a double consonant only shows that the vowel before it is short. Miete (rent, long i) vs. Mitte (middle, short i). Don't hold the tt.
Final -eGerman final -e is a short, weak sound (like the e in "the"), not a clear Italian e: Schule = „shu-le", Name = „na-me". And -er at the end sounds like a soft a: Lehrer = „le-ra".
2 · Satzbau · ordine delle parole

Italian: free order. German: the verb is always 2nd

In a German main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2, whatever comes first. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Oggi lavoro.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2 and the subject moves behind it. Never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Oggi devo lavorare. (the infinitive goes to the end)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Mi alzo alle 7. The prefix auf- jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Italien?Viene dall'Italia? Italian uses only intonation; German puts the verb first.
Always a subjectItalian drops the subject: lavoro = I work. German never does: ich arbeite, es regnet (piove), es ist kalt (fa freddo).
Sie, du, ihrSie = Lei, always with a capital S, but with the "they" verb: Sie sind (not „Sie ist"). du = tu. ihr = voi: ihr seid.
3 · Artikel & Genus · il genere

Italian has two genders. German has three, and they often don't match

You already know il and la. German adds a neuter (das), and the genders often differ from Italian. Learn every noun with its article.

4 · Fälle · i casi

Your lo, gli and di, in German

Italian shows roles with word order, prepositions (a, di) and pronouns (lo, gli). German changes the article before the noun.

Like ItalianGerman has "a me" sentences: Das gefällt mir = mi piace, Mir ist kalt = ho freddo, Mir geht es gut = sto bene. mir = mi / a me, dir = ti / a te.

Reading practice

Scan for key words before you read the statement. Negations (nicht, kein, nur, geschlossen, verboten) are the most common traps.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Marco Rossi ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Neapel in Italien. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Marco wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Il tuo amico Marco vuole iscriversi a un corso di tedesco. Scrivi i 5 dati che mancano.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (nome) and Familienname / Nachname (cognome). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Italien", with no article. Germans use German names for many Italian cities: Neapel = Napoli, Mailand = Milano, Rom = Roma, Florenz = Firenze, Venedig = Venezia.
