Warum Spanischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

Spanish and German are both Indo-European, and German has borrowed thousands of words from Latin, the mother of Spanish: Universität, Familie, Information, Kultur. You already know grammatical gender, formal address (usted = Sie), reflexive verbs (me lavo = ich wasche mich) and "tengo hambre" (ich habe Hunger). Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · pronunciación

German sounds, written the Spanish way

Spanish is written as it sounds; German often uses several letters for one sound. Read the right-hand column as Spanish and you will be close.

GermanSounds likeExampleRead it as
Achtung!German h is pronounced at the start of a word: Haus, Hallo, heute (like a soft Spanish j). Spanish h is silent, so this is the most common mistake. But after a vowel h is silent and makes the vowel long: Uhr, Zahl.
Achtung!b and v are two sounds in German. w is a v with the lower lip on the teeth (Wasser), v is usually f (Vater = fáter), and b is a real b. And s before a vowel buzzes like a bee (Sonne = zone, with an English z). Two new vowels: ü (say i with rounded lips) and ö (say e with rounded lips).
Long and shortGerman vowels can be long or short, and it changes the meaning: Staat (state) vs. Stadt (city), Miete (rent) vs. Mitte (middle). A double vowel, ie or h means long; a double consonant means short.
2 · Satzbau · orden de la frase

Spanish: free order. German: the verb is always 2nd

Spanish word order is flexible. German is strict: in a main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2, whatever comes first. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Trabajo hoy.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2 and the subject moves behind it. Never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Tengo que trabajar hoy. (the infinitive goes to the end)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Me levanto a las 7. The prefix auf- jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Spanien?¿Es usted de España? Yes/no question: verb first.
Always a subjectSpanish drops the subject: trabajo = I work. German never does: ich arbeite, es regnet (llueve), es ist kalt (hace frío). Every German sentence needs ich, du, er, es…
Sie = ustedFormal Sie is your usted, but it takes the "they" verb (Sie sind, like ustedes son) and always has a capital S. ihr = vosotros in Spain and the informal ustedes in Latin America: ihr seid = vosotros sois / ustedes son.
3 · Artikel & Genus · el género

Spanish has two genders. German has three, and they often don't match.

You already know that nouns have gender: el libro, la mesa. German adds a third, neuter (das), and the genders often differ from Spanish. Learn every noun with its article.

4 · Fälle · los casos

Your lo, le and de, in German

Spanish shows roles with word order and prepositions (a, de) and with pronouns (lo, le). German changes the article before the noun.

Reading practice

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Carlos Martínez ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Medellín in Kolumbien. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Carlos wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Tu amigo Carlos quiere inscribirse en un curso de alemán. Escribe los 5 datos que faltan.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (nombre) and Familienname / Nachname (apellido). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country names usually have no article: „aus Kolumbien", „aus Spanien", „aus Mexiko". Write both apellidos as in the passport.
