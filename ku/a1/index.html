Was Kurdischsprachige schon können

Your head start: Latin letters, verb at the end, genders

Kurmanji and German are distant cousins in the Indo-European family: nav = Name, nû = neu, bira = Bruder, sê = drei. You already read the Latin alphabet, your verbs already change for each person (ez diçim, tu diçî, em diçin), you already know two genders (mala min, bavê min) and you already put the verb at the end (Ez îro dixebitim). What is new: a third gender and articles (der, die, das) in front of every noun. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · bilêvkirin

German sounds, written in Kurdish letters

Read the right-hand column as Kurmanji. German stresses the first syllable of most words: Ar-beit, Fa-mi-lie is an exception (Fa-mi-lie).

GermanSounds likeExampleRead it as
Achtung!Same letters, different sounds. German z = ts (Zeit = „tsayt"), j = Kurdish y (ja = „ya"), w = Kurdish v (Wasser = „vaser"), v = f (Vater = „fater"), y = ü, and s before a vowel = Kurdish z (Sonne = „zone"). German c appears only in ch, ck and loanwords: never read it as Kurdish c (= dsch).
Your bonusKurdish x is exactly German ch in Buch, acht, Nacht. And German r at the start of a word comes from the throat, close to the soft Kurdish gh (ẍ). Only ö and ü are new: say ê or î and round your lips.
2 · Satzbau · rêzika hevokê

Kurmanji: verb at the end. German: verb 2nd, the rest at the end

Kurmanji puts the verb last: Ez îro dixebitim. In a German main clause the conjugated verb stands in position 2. But every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end, exactly where Kurmanji puts it.

Ich arbeite heute.Ez îro dixebitim.
Heute arbeite ich.Îro ez dixebitim. If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Divê ez îro bixebitim. The second verb goes to the end.
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Ez saet heftan radibim. „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Syrien?Hûn ji Sûriyeyê ne? Kurmanji asks with the voice (or gelo); German puts the verb first.
Verb endingsYou know this already: ez diçim, tu diçî, ew diçe, em diçin. German: ich gehe, du gehst, er geht, wir gehen. Even the plural ends in -n in both languages!
Sie, du, ihrGerman has du (tu), ihr (hûn, several friends) and Sie (hûn, polite, always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say hûn out of respect.
3 · Artikel & Genus · zayend

der, die, das: you know two genders, German has three

Kurmanji nouns are masculine or feminine, and you hear it in the ezafe: bavê min, mala min. German adds a third gender, neuter, and shows it with an article in front: der Tisch, die Katze, das Buch. The genders often don't match Kurmanji, so learn every noun with its article. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

4 · Fälle · rewş

You already know cases: ez → min, tu → te

Kurmanji changes pronouns and endings: ez → min, jin → jinê. German changes the article and the pronoun: der → den, ich → mich / mir. At A1, focus on Nominativ (who does it) and Akkusativ (what or whom). Only der changes: der → den.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Azad Demir ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Qamischli in Syrien. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Azad wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Hevalê te Azad dixwaze xwe li kursa almanî qeyd bike. 5 agahiyên kêm binivîse.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (nav) and Familienname / Nachname (paşnav). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Syrien", „aus der Türkei", „aus dem Irak", „aus dem Iran". Most countries take no article, but these three do! Write place names as in your documents. Dates are written day.month.year.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Kurdish speakers, because the letter looks the same but sounds different: C is „tsê", H is „ha", I is „î", J is „yot" (not Kurdish j), Q is „kû", R is „er" from the throat, U is „û", V is „faw", W is „vê", X is „îks" (not Kurdish x), Y is „ûpsîlon" and Z is „tset" (not Kurdish z). Tap a letter to hear it.

Kurdish names · navên kurdî

ê, î, û, ş, çGerman keyboards and forms have no ê, î, û, ş or ç. Write the name exactly as in your passport or residence document: Kurdish Şêxmûs may be written Sehmus or Şeyhmus there.
Spelling lettersGermans say „mit Hatschek" or „mit Strich" for special marks. Easier: give the letters as in your passport. Say „Doppel-" for double letters: Hesso = H – E – Doppel-S – O.
Mein Name ist Demir.D – E – M – I – R: „dê – ê – em – î – er" (careful: I = „î", R = „er")
