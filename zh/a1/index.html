Was Chinesischsprachige schon können

Your head start: pinyin

You already know the Latin alphabet from pinyin, and even ü is familiar (绿 lǜ). But German letters often sound different: German z = pinyin c. German has no tones: the melody of a word never changes its meaning. Instead, German has things Chinese doesn't: verb endings, articles (der, die, das), plural endings and cases. The good news: the basic word order (I – work – …) is the same as in Chinese. Tap any to hear the German.

Achtung!Say the last consonant. Mandarin syllables end only in a vowel, -n or -ng. In German you must hear them: Hund = „hun-t", Berg = „béc-k". Many words end in two or three consonants: Herbst, Obst, Wurst. Say every one.
No tonesGerman uses melody only for feeling and for questions: the voice goes up at the end of a yes/no question. Don't worry about „wrong tones": a German word means the same at any pitch.
2 · Satzbau · 语序

Same start as Chinese, but the verb is always 2nd

Chinese and German both start with the subject: Ich arbeite heute = 我今天工作. But in German the conjugated verb must stay in position 2, and a second verb goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.我今天工作。
Heute arbeite ich.今天我工作。Chinese keeps 我 before the verb; German swaps: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.我今天必须工作。The second verb goes to the end.
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.我七点起床。„auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus China?您来自中国吗？No 吗 at the end: the verb goes first.
Verbs changeChinese verbs never change (我去，你去，他们去). German verbs take endings: ich gehe, du gehst, er geht, wir gehen. Learn these four endings first: they work for most verbs.
Sie, du, ihrGerman has du (你, one friend), ihr (你们, several friends) and Sie (您, polite, one or many, always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say 您.
3 · Artikel & Genus · 词性

der, die, das: think of them as 量词

Chinese puts a measure word before the noun: 一张桌子，一只猫，一本书. German puts an article before every noun: der Tisch, die Katze, das Buch. Just like a 量词, you must learn it with each noun. There are three: masculine, feminine and neuter, and they rarely follow logic. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

PluralChinese usually shows no plural (only 们 for people); German changes the noun itself: das Kind → die Kinder, der Tisch → die Tische, die Frau → die Frauen, das Auto → die Autos. Learn the plural with the noun: der Tisch, die Tische.
4 · Fälle · 格

Who does what? German shows it with the article

Chinese shows roles through word order and little words like 给 and 的. German also changes the article. At A1, focus on two cases: Nominativ (who does it) and Akkusativ (what or whom). Only der changes: der → den.

FallGermanChineseClue
Nominativder Mann ist hier.那个男人在这儿。who does it
AkkusativIch sehe den Mann.我看见那个男人。what / whom → den
DativIch gebe dem Mann das Buch.我给那个男人一本书。给 → dem
Genitivdas Auto des Mannes那个男人的车的 → des
Your clueWhere Chinese uses 给 (to someone), German uses the Dativ: dem, der, einem, mir, dir. Where Chinese uses 的, German uses the Genitiv, or simply „von": das Auto von Wei = 伟的车.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Wei Zhang ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Shanghai in China. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Wei wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. 你的朋友张伟想报名参加德语课，请填写缺少的5项信息。

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (名) and Familienname / Nachname (姓). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus China", with no article. Germans write Peking for 北京. Dates are written day.month.year: 01.10.1995 = 1995年10月1日.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Chinese speakers (pinyin spelling): C is „ce" (ts), E is „e" as in „eh", not pinyin e, G is „ge" (hard g), J is „yot" (not pinyin j), R is „er" from the throat, V is „fau", W is „ve", Y is „üpsilon" and Z is „cet" (pinyin c!). Tap a letter to hear it.
