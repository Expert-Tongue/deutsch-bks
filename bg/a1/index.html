Was Bulgarischsprachige schon können

Your head start: three genders and German words you already use

Bulgarian already gives you a lot: three genders (столът, масата, прозорецът), just like German; verbs that change for each person (работя, работиш, работи); a polite Вие (= Sie), also written with a capital letter; and German words you use every day: бира, майстор, шунка, щепсел. Even the sounds help: х = ch in Buch, ц = z, щ = st in Straße. What is new: the article goes in front, cases (der, den, dem), and the verb in position 2. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · произношение

German sounds, written in Cyrillic

Read the right-hand column as Bulgarian. German stresses the first syllable of most words: Ar-beit, Fa-mi-lie is an exception (Fa-mi-lie).

GermanSounds likeExampleRead it as
Achtung!Latin letters that look Cyrillic. German P = п (not р), H = х (not н), C = ц or к (not с), B = б (not в), X = кс (not х), Y = ю (not у). Read every letter as Latin: Paris = „Парис", Hose = „Хозе".
Your bonusBulgarian х is exactly German ch in Buch, acht; ц is German z; щ is German st at the start (Stadt = „щат"); and the weak ъ is the German final -e (bitte = „битъ"). Bulgarian also hardens final b, d, g (хляб = „хляп"), just like German: gelb = „гелп".
2 · Satzbau · словоред

Verb 2nd, and the second verb at the end

Bulgarian and German both often say Ich arbeite heute = Аз работя днес. But in German the conjugated verb must stay in position 2, and every second verb, participle or prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Аз работя днес.
Heute arbeite ich.Днес работя. If something else comes first, the verb stays 2nd and „ich" moves behind it: never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Трябва да работя днес. The second verb goes to the end, and there is no „да".
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Ставам в 7 часа. „auf" jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Bulgarien?От България ли сте? No „ли": the verb goes first.
Say „ich"Bulgarian often drops the subject (Работя днес), because the verb ending shows the person. German almost never does: Ich arbeite heute. Every German sentence needs a subject, even „es": Es regnet = Вали.
Sie, du, ihrGerman du = ти, ihr = вие (several friends), Sie = Вие (polite, always with a capital S). Use Sie wherever you would say Вие out of respect.
3 · Artikel & Genus · род

der, die, das: three genders, like Bulgarian, but in front

Bulgarian glues the article to the end: мъжът, жената, детето. German puts it in front: der Mann, die Frau, das Kind. Both languages have three genders, and sometimes they match. But often they don't: маса is feminine, der Tisch is masculine. Learn every noun with its article. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

PluralLike Bulgarian (стол → столове, жена → жени), German has several plural forms: das Kind → die Kinder, der Tisch → die Tische, die Frau → die Frauen, das Auto → die Autos. Learn the plural with the noun: der Tisch, die Tische.
4 · Fälle · падежи

Cases: Bulgarian keeps them only in pronouns

Bulgarian nouns have no cases, but the pronouns do: аз → мен, ме, ми. German has cases everywhere, and shows them in the article: der Mann, den Mann, dem Mann. At A1, focus on Nominativ and Akkusativ: only der changes, der → den.

Your clueBulgarian ме / ми work exactly like German mich / mir: Виждаш ли ме? = Siehst du mich? Дай ми! = Gib mir! Where Bulgarian says „на някого" (to someone), German uses the Dativ.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Georgi Petrov ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Plowdiw in Bulgarien. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Georgi wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Приятелят ти Георги иска да се запише на курс по немски. Напиши петте липсващи данни.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (име) and Familienname / Nachname (фамилия). The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country: „aus Bulgarien", with no article. Write names in Latin letters, as in your ID card or passport. Dates are written day.month.year, as in Bulgaria.

Teil 1 · buchstabieren

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps for Bulgarian speakers, because some Latin letters look like Cyrillic ones: B is „бе" (not в), C is „це" (not с), H is „ха" (not н), P is „пе" (not р), X is „икс" (not х). Also: J is „йот", R is „ер" from the throat, V is „фау", W is „ве", Y is „юпсилон" and Z is „цет". Tap a letter to hear it.
