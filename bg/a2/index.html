A2 für Bulgarischsprachige

A2: the past, the verb at the end, and polite forms

A2 brings the spoken past (Perfekt), two-way prepositions, subordinate clauses with the verb at the end, reflexive verbs, comparisons and polite forms. Some of it is easy for you: comparisons work like по-голям от, and polite requests like Бихте ли …?. Learn the patterns below and A2 becomes much easier.

1 · Perfekt · минало време

Ich habe gearbeitet = Работих / Работил съм

Bulgarian builds its perfect with съм (работил съм). German uses a helper verb, haben for most verbs and sein for movement or change, in position 2, and the participle at the end. In everyday speech Germans use this Perfekt where Bulgarians use the aorist (работих).

GermanБългарскиHelperWhy
Ich habe Kaffee getrunken.Пих кафе.habenmost verbs
Wir haben Deutsch gelernt.Учихме немски.habenmost verbs
Ich bin nach Hamburg gefahren.Пътувах до Хамбург.seinmovement A → B
Er ist spät aufgestanden.Той стана късно.seinchange of state
Sie ist zu Hause geblieben.Тя остана вкъщи.seinexception: bleiben, sein
ShapeMost participles have ge- at the front and -t or -en at the end: ge-mach-t, ge-trunk-en. Verbs ending in -ieren have no ge-: telefoniert, studiert.

Präteritum for the most common verbs

2 · Wechselpräpositionen · накъде? къде?

Akkusativ = movement, Dativ = place

Bulgarian uses the same preposition for both: отивам в града (going) and в града съм (being). German keeps the preposition but changes the article: Wohin? (накъде?) → Akkusativ, Wo? (къде?) → Dativ.

Wohin? → Akkusativнакъде?Wo? → Dativкъде?
Ich gehe in die Stadt.Отивам в града.Ich bin in der Stadt.В града съм.
Ich lege das Buch auf den Tisch.Слагам книгата на масата.Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.Книгата е на масата.
Wir fahren an den See.Отиваме на езерото.Wir sind am See.На езерото сме.
Die Katze läuft unter das Bett.Котката бяга под леглото.Die Katze schläft unter dem Bett.Котката спи под леглото.
Nine wordsin, an, auf, unter, über, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen. Ask yourself: „накъде?" (movement) → Akkusativ; „къде?" (place) → Dativ. „Ich gehe ins Kino" vs. „Ich bin im Kino".
3 · Nebensätze · подчинени изречения

After weil, dass, wenn: the verb goes to the end

After защото, че or ако, Bulgarian keeps normal order. In a German subordinate clause the conjugated verb jumps to the last position. This is the biggest new habit at A2.

Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin.Няма да дойда, защото съм болен.
Ich glaube, dass er heute kommt.Мисля, че той ще дойде днес.
Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an.Ако имам време, ще ти се обадя.
Ich weiß nicht, ob sie zu Hause ist.Не знам дали тя е вкъщи.
Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren.Въпреки че вали, отиваме на разходка.
Verb, comma, verbIf the sentence starts with the clause, the main clause starts with its verb: „Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an." Two verbs meet at the comma.
4 · Reflexive Verben · възвратни глаголи

mich, dich, sich = се

Bulgarian uses се for every person: мия се, миеш се, мие се. German uses a reflexive pronoun that changes with the person.

ichduer / sie / eswirihrsie / Sie
wasche michwäschst dichwäscht sichwaschen unswascht euchwaschen sich
мия семиеш семие семием семиете семият се
Achtung!Many verbs with се take sich: sich setzen (сядам), sich beeilen (бързам: no се in Bulgarian!), sich freuen (радвам се), sich erinnern (спомням си), sich fühlen (чувствам се). „Ich fühle mich gut" = Чувствам се добре.

Like BulgarianThe order is the same: adjective, then als (от) + the thing. German just adds -er at the end instead of „по-" at the front. Short words often add an umlaut: alt → älter, groß → größer, jung → jünger.
6 · Höflich sein · учтивост

würde, hätte, könnte = бих, бихте

Bulgarian is polite with Вие and the conditional: Бихте ли …?, бих искал. German does exactly the same with its own conditional forms. They sound much friendlier than the plain present, so use them at offices, at the doctor's and in the exam.

Ich hätte gern einen Termin.Бих искал да си запиша час.
Könnten Sie mir helfen?Бихте ли ми помогнали?
Ich würde gern am Montag kommen.Бих искал да дойда в понеделник.
An deiner Stelle würde ich zum Arzt gehen.На твое място бих отишъл на лекар.
Cultural tipCareful with your head! In Bulgaria a nod can mean „no" and a head shake „yes". In Germany a nod always means yes and a shake means no: say „Ja" or „Nein" out loud to be safe. Being on time matters: 5 minutes late is already late.

Lesen · четене

Reading practice

Read the question first, then look for the key words in the text. Watch for nicht, kein, nur, schon, noch and for comparatives (größer, teurer).

Informal or formal?

An Freunde · du · до приятели

Formell · Sie · официално

Achtung!Don't mix du and Sie in one message. Your teacher, your boss and the Ausländerbehörde get Sie / Ihnen / Ihr with a capital letter, to show respect, like Вие. And use at least one weil or deshalb: A2 examiners look for connectors.
Teil 1 · Fragen zur Person

Ask and answer with word cards

You get a card with a question and four prompts. Say 2–3 sentences for each prompt. Use the Perfekt and connectors (zuerst, dann, weil, aber).
