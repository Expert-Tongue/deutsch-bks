Warum A2 für Rumänischsprachige leichter ist

A lot of A2 grammar already exists in Romanian

A2 is the level where German grammar opens up: the past tense, two-way prepositions, subordinate clauses, reflexive verbs, comparisons and polite forms. Romanian already has a past with a helper (am lucrat), reflexive verbs (mă spăl), cases and a conditional for politeness (ați putea, aș vrea). The new part is word order.

1 · Perfekt · perfect compus

Ich habe gearbeitet = am lucrat

This is your perfect compus! Romanian always uses "a avea"; German uses haben for most verbs and sein (a fi) for movement or change. The helper stands in position 2, the participle at the end.

GermanRomanianHelperWhy
Ich habe Kaffee getrunken.Am băut o cafea.habenmost verbs
Wir haben Deutsch gelernt.Am învățat germană.habenmost verbs
Ich bin nach Hamburg gefahren.Am mers la Hamburg.seinmovement A → B
Er ist spät aufgestanden.S-a sculat târziu.seinchange of state
Sie ist zu Hause geblieben.A rămas acasă.seinexception: bleiben, sein
Perfekt or Präteritum?Spoken German uses the Perfekt for almost every past event, just as Romanian uses the perfect compus. Watch out for sein: Romanian says „am mers", German says „ich bin gegangen".

Präteritum for the most common verbs

Ich war in Bukarest.Am fost la București.
Ich hatte keine Zeit.Nu aveam timp.
Ich musste arbeiten.A trebuit să lucrez.
Ich konnte nicht kommen.Nu am putut veni.

2 · Wechselpräpositionen · încotro sau unde?

Akkusativ = movement, Dativ = place

Romanian mostly uses unde for "unde mergi?" and "unde ești?". German has two: Wohin? (movement) → Akkusativ, Wo? (place) → Dativ. Nine prepositions follow this rule, and German changes the article.

Wohin? → AkkusativRomanianWo? → DativRomanian
Ich gehe in die Stadt.Merg în oraș.Ich bin in der Stadt.Sunt în oraș.
Ich lege das Buch auf den Tisch.Pun cartea pe masă.Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.Cartea e pe masă.
Wir fahren an den See.Mergem la lac.Wir sind am See.Suntem la lac.
Die Katze läuft unter das Bett.Pisica fuge sub pat.Die Katze schläft unter dem Bett.Pisica doarme sub pat.
Nine wordsin, an, auf, unter, über, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen. „Pun cartea pe masă" and „cartea e pe masă" use the same word in Romanian. In German, ask: is there movement (wohin?) or not (wo?)?
3 · Nebensätze · propoziții subordonate

After weil, dass, wenn: the verb goes to the end

This is the biggest change for Romanian speakers. After pentru că or că, Romanian keeps the normal order. In a German subordinate clause the conjugated verb goes to the last position.

Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin.Nu vin pentru că sunt bolnav.
Ich glaube, dass er heute kommt.Cred că vine azi.
Wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an.Dacă am timp, te sun.
Ich weiß nicht, ob sie zu Hause ist.Nu știu dacă e acasă.
Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren.Deși plouă, mergem la plimbare.
Verb, comma, verbIf the sentence starts with the clause (Wenn ich Zeit habe, …), the main clause starts with the verb: „rufe ich dich an". Romanian says "dacă am timp, te sun"; German says „wenn ich Zeit habe, rufe ich dich an".
4 · Reflexive Verben · verbe reflexive

mich, dich, sich = mă, te, se

Good news: this works almost exactly like Romanian. Only the position differs: Romanian puts mă / te / se before the verb, German puts mich / dich / sich after it.

ichduer / sie / eswirihrsie / Sie
wasche michwäschst dichwäscht sichwaschen unswascht euchwaschen sich

Achtung!Not every verb matches: sich beeilen = a se grăbi ✓, sich erinnern = a-și aminti ✓, but aufstehen = a se scula has no sich in German. Learn each verb with or without sich.
5 · Komparation · comparația

Unlike RomanianRomanian always adds „mai"; German adds -er to the word, and a few are irregular: gut → besser, viel → mehr, gern → lieber. Short words often add an umlaut: alt → älter, groß → größer, jung → jünger.
6 · Höflich sein · politețe

würde, hätte, könnte = condiționalul

Romanian is polite with the conditional: ați putea…?, aș vrea…, mi-ar plăcea…. German does exactly the same with three short verbs. They sound much friendlier than the present tense, so use them at offices, at the doctor's and in the exam.

Ich hätte gern einen Termin.Aș vrea o programare.
Könnten Sie mir helfen?Ați putea să mă ajutați?
Ich würde gern am Montag kommen.Mi-ar plăcea să vin luni.
An deiner Stelle würde ich zum Arzt gehen.În locul tău, aș merge la doctor.
Cultural tipGerman communication is direct: „Nein" is not rude, and Germans say clearly what they think. Small talk is shorter, people keep more physical distance, and greetings are a handshake, not kisses on the cheek. Be on time: 5 minutes late is already late.

Reading practice

Read the question first, then look for the key words in the text. Watch for nicht, kein, nur, schon, noch and for comparatives (größer, teurer).
