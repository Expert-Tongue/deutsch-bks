Warum Russischsprachige Vorteile haben

You already know more German than you think

Russian already has three genders, cases (German has four, you have six), the same final-consonant rule (Hund = „хунт", like хлеб = „хлеп") and many German loanwords: бутерброд, рюкзак, бухгалтер, штраф, шлагбаум, ландшафт. Tap any to hear the German.

1 · Aussprache · произношение

German sounds, written in Cyrillic

Many German spellings are Russian sounds in disguise: German sch = ш, tsch = ч, z = ц, w = в, ch = х. Read the right-hand column as Russian and you will be very close.

Achtung!German ei = ай, eu = ой, ie = долгое и: mein = „майн", heute = „хойте", Liebe = „лиибе". And German e never softens the consonant before it: Tee = „тее", not „тье"; Peter = „петер", not „пьетер".
Long vowelsRussian has no long vowels; German does, and they change the meaning: Staat (state) vs. Stadt (city), Miete (rent) vs. Mitte (middle). A double vowel, ie or h means long. And two new vowels: ü (say и with rounded lips, not „ю") and ö (say э with rounded lips, not „ё").
Clear oRussian turns unstressed o into a („молоко" = „малако"). German doesn't: Problem, Moment, Kollege keep a clear o. And German usually stresses the first syllable of the stem: AR-bei-ten, FAH-ren.
2 · Satzbau · порядок слов

Russian: free order. German: the verb is always 2nd

Thanks to cases, Russian word order is free. German is strict: in a main clause the conjugated verb is in position 2, whatever comes first. A second verb, a participle or a separable prefix goes to the end.

Ich arbeite heute.Я работаю сегодня.
Heute arbeite ich.Time first? The verb stays in position 2 and the subject moves behind it. Never „Heute ich arbeite".
Ich muss heute arbeiten.Я должен сегодня работать. (the infinitive goes to the end)
Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.Я встаю в 7. The prefix auf- jumps to the end.
Kommen Sie aus Russland?Вы из России? Russian uses intonation; German puts the verb first.
Always a verb and a subjectRussian says „я дома" with no verb and „холодно" with no subject. German needs both: Ich bin zu Hause, es ist kalt, es regnet (идёт дождь).
Sie = ВыGerman Sie works like your polite Вы, with a capital S and a plural verb: Sie sind. ihr = вы (plural, informal): ihr seid.
3 · Artikel & Genus · род

Three genders, like Russian, but German shows them with an article

Russian has no articles: the ending shows the gender (дом, женщина, окно). German puts der, die or das in front. Often the gender matches, but not always.

Two articlesGerman also has ein / eine (a, one) and der / die / das (the). Russian has neither, so you will forget them at first. Rule of thumb: first mention = ein, already known = der. -ung, -heit, -keit = always die, -chen = always das.

Read the question first, then press „Anhören". Your browser reads the text in German. Use „Text" only after you have answered. In Teil 2 you hear the text only once in the real exam: focus on numbers and on nicht / kein.

Lesen · чтение

Reading practice

Scan for key words before you read the statement. Negations (nicht, kein, nur, geschlossen, verboten) are the most common traps.

Teil 1 · Formular

Fill in the form for your friend

Ihr Freund Dmitri Iwanow ist 32 Jahre alt und kommt aus Almaty in Kasachstan. Er wohnt jetzt in der Königstraße 12, 70173 Stuttgart. Er ist Elektriker von Beruf. Er arbeitet tagsüber und möchte einen Deutschkurs am Abend machen.

Dmitri wants to register at the Volkshochschule. Write the 5 missing details. Твой друг Дмитрий хочет записаться на курс немецкого. Впиши 5 недостающих данных.

Achtung!German forms use Vorname (имя) and Familienname / Nachname (фамилия). There is no field for the patronymic (отчество).. The postcode (PLZ) comes before the city: 70173 Stuttgart. Country names usually have no article: „aus Kasachstan", „aus Russland". Exceptions: „aus der Ukraine", „aus der Republik Moldau".

Spell your name the German way

You will be asked to spell your name. Orange letters are traps if you learned the English alphabet: A is „a", E is „e" (not „и"), H is „ha", I is „i", J is „jot", R is „er", V is „fau", W is „we", Y is „üpsilon", Z is „tset". Tap a letter to hear it.
